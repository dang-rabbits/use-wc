use-layout is a configurable flex-container primitive — like use-card, it's styled by tag name but isn't a registered custom element, and needs the design-system stylesheet loaded. Attributes drive the flex container: direction (column default, or row), align, justify, gap (a named scale, xsmall…super), wrap, and inline.

By default use-layout doesn't touch its children. Give one direct child the fill attribute and that child grows to consume the remaining space while every other child is pinned to a fixed size. use-layout takes fill itself, so a nested growing (and scrolling) region needs no separate tag.

Adding class="shell" layers on the full shell convention: every child is fixed even when no fill child is present, and the fill region scrolls its own overflow. This is the same mechanic the surface hosts below (dialog, use-card, [popover]) use directly. direction="row" still overrides the column default.

Surfaces

dialog, use-card, and [popover] are the shell layout applied directly — no wrapper needed, since they're already the flex container an author nests header / [fill] / footer into. They also share a header/footer/figure region treatment (avatar sizing, header/footer justification, a footer rule) that a bare .shell doesn't get. use-card is a theme-only tag with no registration — an author can still add role/ARIA to it directly, just not for free the way article/section give an implicit landmark.

A header aligns its contents to the inline-start edge, a footer to the inline-end edge — so a lone trailing action in a footer, or a title plus a trailing action in a header (via hgroup), lands where you'd expect with no per-child margin. Wrap groups of items in <section>s to take over: two or more <section> children switch the region to space-between, dropping one group at each edge.
